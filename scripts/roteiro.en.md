# Two windows, one page

[SCREEN: control panel]

The same file opens in two ways. With nothing at the end of the address, it's the control panel. With `#prompter` at the end, it's the screen that goes to the Elgato Prompter. The two talk through the browser itself, so whatever you change in the panel shows up on the prompter right away.

To get started, click "Open prompter". Drag the new window to the Prompter's display and press F for full screen. If the text shows up reversed on the glass, turn on "Mirror" in the settings.

# Auto scroll

[SCREEN: control deck, speed]

In auto mode the text scrolls at a fixed speed. Space plays and pauses. The up and down arrows change the speed, and the side arrows jump from block to block. Reached the end? Play again starts over from the top.

# Follow my voice

[SCREEN: panel with the transcript showing]

Now the mode we use the most. Choose "Follow my voice" and press play. The browser asks for microphone permission and the text starts following what you say.

If I skip a whole sentence, it looks for where I am and carries on from there. If I stop to improvise, it waits. And if I go back to reread a passage, it goes back with me.

[PAUSE · IMPROVISE A SENTENCE OFF SCRIPT]

See? It stopped scrolling while I left the text, and picked up again when I came back.

# The pedal

[SCREEN: settings, Pedal tab]

With the Stream Deck Pedal, your hands are free. In the Stream Deck app, each pedal sends a key: left arrow, Space and right arrow. In the settings you can change what each pedal does and record other keys.

You can also connect the pedal straight over USB, through WebHID in Chrome. Then holding the pedal repeats the action, like holding a key, all the way to the start or the end.

# Text and colors

[SCREEN: settings drawer, Text tab]

In the settings, change size and font, text width, the position of the reading line, the colors of the text, background and cues, and the fade away from the line. Everything changes in the preview and on the prompter at the same time.

# Camera marker

[SCREEN: red reticle on the preview]

The marker shows where the lens sits behind the glass. Turn it on in the Camera tab, pick reticle, ring or dot, and drag it into place. The "Reading line at camera height" button lines the two up, so your eyes stay on the lens while you read.

# The script in Markdown

[SCREEN: Script screen, editor and preview]

The text is a plain Markdown file. A hash sign becomes a section title, like these chapters: the prompter shows them, but the voice doesn't wait for them. Text in square brackets becomes a cue, like the screen notes in this script: it stands out and also stays out of the speech. Three dashes mark a pause, and HTML comments disappear from the prompter.

To bring in a script you already have, drag the .md file onto the editor. To keep it, use "Save .md".
